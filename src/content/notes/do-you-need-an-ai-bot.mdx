Probably not. That is the honest starting position, and I say it as someone who
builds them.

Assistants are genuinely good now in a way they were not three years ago. They
are also the thing every agency is selling, which means a lot of businesses are
being sold one for problems it does not solve.

Here is how to tell which you are.

## What an assistant is actually good at

One thing, mostly: answering questions whose answers already exist in your
material, at a volume or an hour that makes a person expensive.

That is a narrow description and it is meant to be. Inside it, they work well:

- Repeat customer questions, especially out of hours, which is where most
  enquiries are lost
- Looking things up for staff who would otherwise interrupt someone who knows
- Taking an enquiry and turning it into a structured booking or lead
- Drafting the first version of a reply you send twenty times a week

The common thread is that the answer is already written down somewhere. The
assistant is a faster way to find it, not a source of new knowledge.

## Three tests

**Volume.** Are you answering the same handful of questions dozens of times a
week? Under that, a decent FAQ page costs an afternoon and beats a bot. Over it,
the maths changes.

**Written knowledge.** Does the answer exist somewhere, in a price list, a
policy, a set of past replies? If it only exists in your head, an assistant
cannot read your head. Part of the job becomes writing it down, and that work is
worth doing whether or not you build anything.

**Consequence.** What happens if it gets one wrong? A wrong opening hour is
embarrassing. A wrong dosage, price quote, or legal answer is not. The higher
the consequence, the more it should hand over rather than answer.

Two out of three and it is probably worth talking about. One out of three and it
is not.

## What it costs

Roughly, in 2026:

- Off-the-shelf chat widgets: $50 to $500 a month depending on volume
- Something custom, built on your own material: commonly $1,000 to $5,000 to
  build simply, more when it has to connect to your systems
- Running costs: usually tens of dollars a month, not hundreds, at small
  business volumes

I build the custom kind at $5,000 to $10,000, because in practice the work is
rarely just the assistant. It is your material, cleaned up and structured well
enough for anything to answer from, and then the connections to wherever the
enquiry needs to end up.

If your case is simple, buy the widget. I will tell you that on the call and you
will have spent thirty minutes to save several thousand dollars.

## The failure everyone worries about, and the one they should

The worry is that it invents answers. That is a real risk and a solvable one:
you ground it in your documents, you make it cite where an answer came from, and
you make refusal the default when it cannot find one. Done properly, "I do not
know, here is a human" is a feature rather than an embarrassment.

The failure people do not expect is subtler. An assistant trained on material
that is out of date confidently gives out last year's prices. It is not
hallucinating, it is doing exactly what you asked with the wrong source. Which
means the real ongoing cost of an assistant is keeping the material behind it
true.

That is worth understanding before you buy one, because nobody selling a monthly
subscription will mention it.

## The honest recommendation

If you are losing enquiries out of hours and your answers are written down, an
assistant is one of the highest return things you can build.

If you answer six questions a week, or your knowledge lives in one person's
memory, spend the money on the FAQ page and the writing down. It is less exciting
and it will serve you longer.
